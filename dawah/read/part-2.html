Part IIThe Evidence Library

The full evidence bank behind the handbook: every claim the free dawah literature makes, with the verse, the source, and an honest note on how strong it is. Use the handbook to speak; use this Part to prepare and to check.

How to read this library

This material was collected on 27 October 2026 from the free online dawah literature (islam-guide.com and its mirrors on islamreligion.com and IslamHouse, Dr Zakir Naik's The Qur'an and Modern Science, Maurice Bucaille, Harun Yahya, Sapience Institute and Hamza Tzortzis, Yaqeen Institute, Islam Q&A, Bilal Philips) plus academic and sceptic sources for the caveats. The research environment could run web searches but could not open pages in full, so every item was confirmed only through the search engine's quoted extract of the page. Before you print anything, open the source link and check the wording yourself.

Labels used in the tables:

  • S = the claim, the verse and the source were confirmed through a quoted extract of that page.
  • K = standard content of the named dawah book, written from knowledge of that literature; the link is the known location but was not confirmed this time.
  • Unverified = a quote or figure that circulates in dawah material but could not be traced to a primary source. Do not use it in public until you have checked it.
  • Drop = a popular item that careful Muslim scholars themselves reject or that has been shown false. Leave it out.

Honesty rules for using evidence in dawah. These come from the dawah scholars themselves (Hamza Tzortzis's 2013 essay withdrawing the “scientific miracles” approach, Yasir Qadhi's warning that over-reading miracles “makes Muslims look foolish to non-Muslims”, and Yaqeen's apologetics guidelines):

  1. Say “consistent with”, not “predicted”. A 7th-century text that makes no demonstrable scientific error is a defensible claim; “the Quran predicted the Big Bang” is not.
  2. Use the items whose Arabic is plain in any translation (life from water, embryo stages, deep-sea darkness, two seas, milk, three darknesses). Flag the translation-dependent ones (lowest land, egg-shaped earth, expanding, iron sent down) as “possible readings” or leave them out.
  3. Never fight for one example. If a sceptic challenges it, concede and move on. The strength is the pattern.
  4. Do not quote the retracted scientist testimonials (Hay, Kroner). Quote Keith Moore only with context.
  5. Pair science with the stronger evidences: preservation, the literary challenge, prophecy, the Prophet's character, and the arguments for God in the handbook. The dawah organisations now put those first.

সারকথা: টেবিলের S, K, Unverified ও Drop চিহ্নের মানে আর প্রমাণ ব্যবহারের পাঁচটি সততার নিয়ম। মনে রাখো: “ভবিষ্যদ্বাণী করেছে” নয়, “বিজ্ঞানের সঙ্গে মেলে” বলো; একটা উদাহরণ নিয়ে জেদ করো না, আর বাতিল উদ্ধৃতি ব্যবহার করো না।

What Western academics say (quotable)

  • Nicolai Sinai (Oxford), Bulletin of SOAS 77 (2014): “the traditional dating of the standard rasm ... to 650 or earlier ought to be our default view.”

  • Francois Deroche (College de France) dates the Paris codex to 650-675 CE.

  • Quran 15:9 (“We will surely guard it”) is the promise; the manuscripts above are the fulfilment.

The Quran's literary challenge (i'jaz)

The challenge verses: 2:23 and 10:38 (produce one surah like it), 11:13 (ten surahs), 17:88 (humans and jinn together cannot produce its like), 52:33-34. The challenge was issued to the masters of Arabic, who had every motive to answer it, and it was never met.

How Hamza Tzortzis presents it (“The Qur'an's Unique Literary Form”; The Divine Reality chapter 13): classical Arabic has two forms, poetry (sixteen metres) and prose (rhymed saj' or plain). The Quran fits neither; it is a new literary form, and the inability of any Arab in 1,400 years to produce its like is the miracle. He stresses this is a claim to be tested by Arabic experts, so for a non-Arab audience it rests on testimony.

Literary features commonly cited:

  • Ring composition of Surah 2: Raymond Farrin, The Muslim World 100 (2010) and Structure and Qur'anic Interpretation (2014), finds nine concentric sections mirroring each other around the centre. (The popular claim that verse 2:143 is the exact centre is unverified; cite Farrin's structure, not the verse number.)
  • Surah 55: the refrain “Which of your Lord's favours will you both deny?” occurs 31 times in a precise pattern.
  • Surah 108 (al-Kawthar): ten words, three verses, cited by classical scholars as a complete miracle in miniature; al-Razi disagreed that short surahs carry the challenge. Present as a taste, not as proof.
  • Word-count “pairs”: drop (see above).

Constantinople will be conquered; “what a wonderful leader and army” Musnad Ahmad; al-Hakim's Mustadrak 4:422, graded sahih by al-Hakim and al-Dhahabi Grading contested by some scholars Mehmed II, 1453.
Suraqa ibn Malik will wear the bracelets of Khosrau al-Bayhaqi, Dala'il al-Nubuwwa (mursal chain) Not in Bukhari or Muslim Umar placed them on his wrists. A beloved story; state the grading.
“This matter will reach wherever night and day reach” Often cited as Musnad Ahmad 16957 Unverified reference this time; check before using —

How to present prophecy to a sceptic: choose the ones whose text is in Bukhari or Muslim (authenticated centuries before fulfilment, with manuscripts) and whose fulfilment is undeniable: the fall of Persia, the spread to east and west, and the tall buildings. Say the grading of the others.

The Prophet Muhammad as evidence

🔊 Listen to this section · শুনুন

His life (with references)

  • He died with his armour mortgaged to a Jewish neighbour for thirty measures of barley (Sahih al-Bukhari 2916). Months passed with no cooking fire in his house; the family lived on dates and water (Sahih al-Bukhari 6458-6459).
  • Mecca's leaders offered him wealth, leadership and marriage to stop preaching; he refused (sirah of Ibn Ishaq / Ibn Hisham, the Utbah ibn Rabi'ah episode). The famous “sun in my right hand, moon in my left” wording is from the sirah, not a graded hadith; say so.
  • At the conquest of Mecca (630) he gave a general amnesty to the city that had tortured and expelled his followers, pardoning Abu Sufyan, Hind and Ikrimah among others.
  • He was called al-Amin, “the Trustworthy”, before prophethood; even his enemies deposited valuables with him.
  • The argument (W. Montgomery Watt, Muhammad at Mecca, Oxford 1953, p. 52): “His readiness to undergo persecutions for his beliefs, the high moral character of the men who believed in him ... and the greatness of his ultimate achievement, all argue his fundamental integrity. To suppose Muhammad an impostor raises more problems than it solves.” (Verified.)

Historical-accuracy claims and their caveats

These are popular on YouTube. Each has a real point and a real weakness; know both.

Claim Dawah presentation Caveats Verdict
Pharaoh's body preserved as a sign (10:92) Maurice Bucaille examined the mummy of Ramesses II in Paris in 1976 and presented the body's survival as fulfilment; islamreligion.com and Arab News retell his conversion Egyptologists do not identify which pharaoh was the Exodus pharaoh (Ramesses II died of old age at about 90; Merneptah's CT scans show no drowning); classical tafsir reads the verse as the body being cast ashore for the Israelites to see; Bucaille's conversion is itself contested Use the verse's own point: the Quran says the body was saved, and pharaohs' bodies did survive, unlike the Bible's silence. Do not name a specific mummy or claim drowning evidence.
Haman in Egypt (28:6, 28:38) The name appears on an Egyptian stela (Vienna) with a title linked to construction, unknown before hieroglyphs were deciphered Egyptologists read the name differently (built on the god Hemen) and date the man to another period; the identification is not accepted Weak. Present at most as “the Quran places Haman in Egypt, and the Bible's Haman is Persian; the Quran did not copy the Bible”.
“King” in Joseph's story, “Pharaoh” in Moses's story The title Pharaoh became a royal title only in the New Kingdom; the Quran uses “king” for Joseph's time and “Pharaoh” for Moses's, where Genesis uses “Pharaoh” for both Shows consistency, not independent proof; chronology of Joseph is open Good minor point; Yaqeen Institute uses it.
Lowest land (30:3) The Byzantine defeat occurred near the Dead Sea, the lowest dry land on earth Classical reading is “nearest land”; the double meaning is lexical, not consensus Present only as a possible double meaning.
Iram of the Pillars (89:6-8) = Ubar, Oman Nicholas Clapp's 1992 satellite-aided expedition found a caravan fortress at Shisr Scholars do not accept the identification; Iram may be a tribe or region Drop.

Be honest about the cause. Pew's Conrad Hackett: “very little of the change in Muslim population size is a result of people becoming Muslim as adults.” The growth is mainly demographic (young population, high birth rate). The honest dawah point is different and stronger: Islam has the highest retention of any major religion, and the unaffiliated gain mostly from Christianity, not from Islam. People who are born Muslim overwhelmingly stay Muslim.
